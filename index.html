About

No pretending. Just the process.

Husza György (George) · Sighișoara, Romania

My fitness journey

I’ve been training for roughly seven years. Not seven perfect years: seven years of progress, setbacks, inconsistent stretches and restarting. I’ve built muscle and I know what real progress feels like. I also know how easy it is to lose it when life gets busy.

What inconsistency taught me

Most people don’t fail because they lack information. They fail because the plan doesn’t fit their life, nobody notices when they stop, and every restart feels like starting from zero. Structure and accountability matter more than the perfect programme.

Why I started BECOMING YOU

After years of doing this for myself, I want to help others too, especially people at the start, or people starting again. I don’t believe in pretending to have everything figured out. I believe in learning, applying, measuring, improving, and being honest about the process.

What I believe about fitness

  • A plan you’ll follow beats a perfect plan you won’t.
  • Progress is measured, not guessed.
  • Restarting is a skill, not a failure.
  • Honest expectations build more trust than big promises.

Current learning & qualifications

I’m currently learning and building practical experience. I do not hold a formal fitness certification, and I’m not a medical professional, physiotherapist or dietitian.

When I complete a course or certification, it will be listed here with the date and a way to verify it.

My commitment

I’ll tell you what I know, what I don’t, and when something is outside my scope. I’ll show up for check-ins, and I’ll expect you to show up too.

Free resource · no sign-up required

The 7-Day Fitness Consistency Starter.

One week to prove to yourself you can show up. This won’t transform your body in seven days, and nothing honest will. It builds the habit that makes everything after it possible.

Before you startThis is general educational information, not medical advice. If you have an injury, a health condition, or haven’t exercised in a long time, check with a doctor first. Stop any exercise that causes pain.

Your week

Day 1 · Training

Full body A

  • Bodyweight squat: 3 × 8–12
  • Incline push-up (hands on a bench/table): 3 × 6–10
  • Glute bridge: 3 × 10–15
  • Dead bug: 3 × 6 each side
  • Rest 60–90 s between sets
Day 2 · Active recovery

Walk + mobility

  • 20–30 min easy walk
  • 5 min gentle mobility: hips, shoulders, upper back
Day 3 · Training

Full body B

  • Reverse lunge: 3 × 6–8 each leg
  • Floor or incline push-up: 3 × as many good reps as you can, leaving 2 in reserve
  • Table or doorframe row: 3 × 8–12
  • Plank: 3 × 20–30 s
Day 4 · Rest

Walk only

  • 20–30 min walk
  • Prioritise sleep tonight
Day 5 · Training

Full body A (again)

  • Repeat Day 1
  • Try one more rep on one exercise than last time, with clean form
Day 6 · Active recovery

Longer walk

  • 30–45 min walk, ideally outside
  • Optional 5 min mobility
Day 7 · Reflect

Review + plan week 2

  • Fill in the reflection below
  • Schedule next week’s three sessions in your calendar

Exercise basics

Bodyweight squat

Feet about shoulder-width, sit hips back and down, chest proud, knees tracking over toes. Stand up through the whole foot.

Incline push-up

Hands on a stable surface, body in a straight line. Lower your chest toward the edge, then press away. Lower surface = harder.

Glute bridge

Lie on your back, knees bent. Drive through heels, squeeze glutes to lift hips, pause, lower slowly.

Dead bug

On your back, arms up, knees at 90°. Lower opposite arm and leg slowly while keeping your lower back gently pressed down.

Reverse lunge

Step one foot back, lower until both knees are bent, push through the front foot to return. Hold a wall for balance if needed.

Table / doorframe row

Hold a sturdy table edge or doorframe, lean back with a straight body, pull your chest toward your hands. Only use something that can’t move.

Plank

Forearms under shoulders, body straight from head to heels, ribs down, glutes squeezed. Quality over time.

Weekly reflection

  1. What did I complete?

    Count sessions and walks. Be honest, not harsh.

  2. What got in the way?

    Time, energy, plans, mood? Name it specifically.

  3. What made it easier?

    Time of day, clothes ready, a friend? Keep that.

  4. One change for week 2

    Just one. Small and specific.

After the seven days

Progressive improvement means doing slightly more over time: one more rep, a slower lowering phase, a harder push-up variation, a few extra minutes of walking. Small and steady beats big and short-lived.

  • Repeat the week, aiming to beat a few numbers
  • When 12 reps feels easy, choose a harder variation
  • Keep the three sessions on the same days each week
  • Missed a day? Do the next one. Don’t restart the week.
